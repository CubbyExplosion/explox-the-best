The full story

What Explox actually is

Explox is a free 3D city-life game that runs entirely in your browser — no download, no install, nothing to buy just to get in the door. You create a character, step out into a real city, and from that moment on the game doesn't hand you a quest line or a goal. It hands you a life, and lets you decide what to do with it.

The city itself is built to actually be lived in, not just looked at. Downtown has real streets and a working Transit Hub. The Mall has an entire Shopping Wing with 200-plus individually themed storefronts. Out past downtown sit the Suburbs, where 40 named neighbors each have their own house, their own car, and their own job — they aren't scenery, they're people you can walk up to, talk to, befriend, and build a real relationship with over time.

Money in Explox is called S.I.P., and there is no single "correct" way to earn it. Walk into almost any of the 300-plus shops in the city and you can clock in on the spot — pay scales with whatever that specific shop actually sells, so a job at a jewelry counter pays differently than one at a corner store. Prefer to be your own boss? Buy one of ten store tiers, physically stock your own shelves, set your own prices, and hire staff. Want something higher-stakes? The City Bank has three real jobs of its own, including standing Guard duty while the Bank comes under an actual robber siege — complete with giant Coin Bot backup you can call in on a cooldown, and real police officers who show up whether you called them or not.

None of this is solitary by default. Explox has real chat, real friendships, and real milestones — you can marry another player's character or one of the Suburbs neighbors, start a family, and watch a baby actually grow up through real childhood stages over time you spend playing. Elders live out their years and pass on peacefully; friends never do. Birthdays, weddings, and other life events aren't cutscenes, they're things that happen in your city while you're standing in it.

Making your character feel like yours goes deep — hair, hats, skin tones, shirts, pants, shoes, and full outfit sets, all mixable into a look nobody else has. Beyond appearance, you can buy land, build and furnish a real house room by room, own cars, adopt pets, and craft items out of wood, scrap, and rarer materials you gather yourself instead of just buying everything outright.

Explox also has real danger, and you get to decide exactly how much of it you want. Killers, rogue robots, and robbers roam the world and will fight back if you engage them — or ignore you, if that's not the kind of day you're having. A Difficulty picker runs from a gentle "Baby Steps" all the way up to "Apocalypse," and a dedicated Peaceful mode switches off ambient danger entirely for anyone who'd rather build a quiet life than a violent one. Go looking for trouble anyway and there's a real economy of weapons, armor, and consequences waiting, including actual prison time if the police catch up to you.

The world stretches well past the city limits. Book a real flight at the City Airport to any of eight countries — Japan, France, Brazil, Egypt, the UK, Australia, Canada, and Italy — each with its own landmarks, shops, and even its own time zone, so the sky over Tokyo genuinely doesn't match the sky over Rio. Push further and you'll find zones that leave Earth behind entirely: the Moon, Mars, Jupiter, a distant outpost called Andromeda, and stranger destinations tied to the game's own ongoing story that are best discovered rather than spoiled here.

When you want a break from city life without actually leaving the game, Explox has a growing arcade of mini-games reachable in a couple of clicks — everything from Capture the Throne and Robot War to Tower Defense, Obby, Rooftop Parkour, and a whole shelf of smaller classics like 2048, Pong, Blackjack, and Tic-Tac-Toe. New ones get added regularly, and none of them require you to save and quit your main character to play.

The city keeps moving even when you're not actively pushing a button. Day turns to night and back again on a real cycle, weather shifts between clear skies, rain, storms, snow, fog, and breezy leaf-fall depending on the season — and as of the latest update, every player sees the exact same sky and the exact same weather at the exact same moment, so if you and a friend are standing in the same spot, you're standing under the same clouds.

Explox is built and updated by SIP (Super Important Projects), a group that includes Cubby Explosion, adding new content in public one feature at a time rather than shipping once and walking away — which is also why the list above will keep getting longer. It's free to play today, it's meant to stay that way, and the fastest way to actually understand any of this is to stop reading about it and go create a character.

Behind the scenes

How Was It Made?

The world. Explox is drawn live in your browser with Three.js, a library that turns plain JavaScript into real 3D graphics. There's no game engine to install and no app store — the whole game is ordinary web code, split into many separate files that load together when you press Play.

The online part. Accounts, saved progress, chat, the shared weather and the other players you see walking around all run through a small Node.js server with a MongoDB database. Your browser checks in with it about once a second, which is how everyone ends up seeing the same city at the same time.

The people behind it. Explox is made by a group called SIP, which stands for Super Important Projects. Cubby Explosion decides what Explox needs next and play-tests it, Hubby also helps with the code and the sound, and gurnaldst made the real soundtracks. That's how the game keeps growing one feature at a time instead of arriving all at once.

🎵 The real soundtracks were made by gurnaldst

Explox has 57 music tracks, and they come from two different places. The 50 background tracks are synthesized live by the game's own code using the browser's Web Audio — there's no recording behind them. The 7 real soundtracks are actual songs, and every one of them was made by gurnaldst:
